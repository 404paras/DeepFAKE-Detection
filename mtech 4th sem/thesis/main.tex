\chapter*{Abstract}
% Note: \addcontentsline keeps Abstract listed in the ToC
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
The proliferation of generative models—spanning GANs, autoencoders, and diffusion-based pipelines—has led to highly realistic deepfakes that challenge forensic detection. This thesis addresses two complementary aspects of the problem through a unified research agenda. First, a hybrid \textit{video-only} detector is developed that integrates spatial and temporal reasoning to identify visual forgeries. The architecture leverages ResNet50 for frame-level spatial encoding, Bidirectional GRUs (Bi-GRUs) for temporal modeling across sequences, and a Multi-Head Attention mechanism to focus on salient frame segments. Trained on an aggregated benchmark of FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF, and the Deepfake Detection Challenge (DFDC), the model exhibits strong generalization with Accuracy \(\approx 0.927\), AUC \(\approx 0.925\), and balanced precision–recall, and demonstrates robustness to dataset shift and reductions in fairness disparities across demographic subgroups.

Building on insights from Phase I, the second contribution advances to \textit{multimodal} audio-visual detection with explicit modeling of biological synchrony between visemes and phonemes. Contemporary deepfakes increasingly coordinate both modalities, yet often fail to reproduce fine-grained temporal correlations inherent to natural speech production. A dual-stream framework is proposed wherein a ResNet50-based visual encoder and a convolutional audio encoder are each augmented with Bi-GRUs to capture modality-specific dynamics. An 8-head cross-modal attention layer then aligns and contrasts spectro-temporal and spatio-temporal representations to expose short-duration audio-visual misalignments indicative of manipulation. Evaluated on a consolidated corpus of 28{,}461 videos from FakeAVCeleb and AV-Deepfake1M++, the multimodal system achieves 92.5\% Accuracy and an AUC-ROC of 0.94, outperforming unimodal and naïve fusion baselines. Additional studies assess robustness under lossy compression and examine demographic fairness.

Together, these contributions show that (i) hybrid spatial–temporal modeling enhances generalization for video-only detection, and (ii) explicit synchronicity reasoning via cross-modal attention and bidirectional temporal modeling substantially improves resilience against modern multimodal forgeries. The thesis concludes with a discussion of deployment considerations, ethical aspects, and future directions including transformer-based fusion, resolution-invariant features, adversarial robustness, and cross-lingual audio-visual deepfake detection.
